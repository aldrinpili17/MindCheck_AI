Grounding exercises

Simple techniques to interrupt racing thoughts and bring you back to the present moment.

5 Senses Technique (5‑4‑3‑2‑1 Method)

Engages all five senses to interrupt racing thoughts.

  • 5 things you can see — look around and name five distinct items (a clock, a pattern on the wall, a tree outside).
  • 4 things you can touch — feel four textures near you (fabric of your pants, a cool desk surface, the edge of a chair).
  • 3 things you can hear — notice three ambient sounds (distant traffic, a fan humming, birds chirping).
  • 2 things you can smell — identify two scents (coffee brewing, soap, fresh air).
  • 1 thing you can taste — focus on one taste (a mint, a sip of water, or simply the current taste in your mouth).
Physical & body-based grounding

Uses movement and physical contact to bring awareness back to the body.

  • Feet on the floor — sit comfortably, uncross your legs, place both feet flat on the ground, and press down firmly. Focus on the connection between your soles and the floor.
  • Progressive muscle relaxation — tense a muscle group (shoulders or fists) tightly for 5 seconds, then fully release. Notice the contrast between tension and relaxation.
Cognitive grounding

Re-engages the rational, analytical part of your brain.

  • Category naming — pick a broad category (dog breeds, cities, movies, fruits) and name 10 items as quickly as you can.
  • The alphabet game — pick a topic (animals, foods) and name one item starting with each letter, A to Z.
  • Mental math — count backward from 100 by 7s, or recite a favorite poem, song lyric, or quote backward.
Breathing exercises

Controlled breathing directly stimulates the parasympathetic nervous system to slow your heart rate.

  • Box breathing — inhale through the nose for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, exhale through the mouth for 4 seconds, hold empty for 4 seconds. Repeat for 3–4 cycles.
  • 4‑7‑8 breathing — inhale quietly through the nose for 4 seconds, hold for 7 seconds, exhale completely through the mouth (a soft "whoosh" sound) for 8 seconds.
